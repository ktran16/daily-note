## 00:02 - [Data Engineering] Dagster Software-Defined Assets: Orchestrating Data, Not Tasks

Airflow asks "what tasks should run, in what order?" Dagster's asset model asks a different question: "what data objects should exist, and how is each one produced?" You declare the *asset* — a table, a file, an ML model — and Dagster derives the DAG from the dependencies between assets.

```python
from dagster import asset, AssetExecutionContext

@asset
def raw_orders(context: AssetExecutionContext) -> None:
    df = fetch_from_api()
    context.log.info(f"loaded {len(df)} rows")
    write_to_warehouse("raw.orders", df)

@asset(deps=[raw_orders])
def daily_revenue() -> None:
    run_sql("""
        CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE marts.daily_revenue AS
        SELECT order_date, SUM(amount) AS revenue
        FROM raw.orders GROUP BY 1
    """)
```

No `>>` operators, no `set_upstream`. The dependency is the function argument or `deps` list, so the lineage graph *is* the code.

### Why this matters operationally

The payoff is **selective materialization**. Because Dagster knows each asset's freshness and upstream lineage, you can rebuild one table and everything downstream of it — `dagster asset materialize --select "raw_orders+"` — instead of rerunning a whole schedule. When a stakeholder says "yesterday's revenue looks wrong," you re-materialize that one asset, not the pipeline.

### Partitions are first-class

Backfills stop being a bespoke script:

```python
from dagster import asset, DailyPartitionsDefinition

@asset(partitions_def=DailyPartitionsDefinition(start_date="2026-01-01"))
def events(context) -> None:
    day = context.partition_key          # "2026-08-24"
    run_sql(f"""
        DELETE FROM raw.events WHERE event_date = '{day}';
        INSERT INTO raw.events SELECT * FROM staging WHERE event_date = '{day}';
    """)
```

Each partition is independently materializable, and the delete-then-insert makes reruns idempotent. Dagster tracks which partitions are missing or stale and shows them as a grid in the UI — a backfill is selecting a range, not writing a for-loop.

### The trade-off

Task-shaped work that produces no data object (send an email, kick a Spark cluster) fits awkwardly; Dagster has `@op`/`@job` for that, so mixed codebases carry two mental models. And the asset abstraction only earns its keep if your pipeline really is a graph of tables. For a handful of scripts on a cron, Airflow's simpler model is still fine.

## 05:03 - [Data Analytics] Date Spines: The Fix for Silently Missing Rows

A `GROUP BY order_date` only returns dates that *have* orders. Days with zero activity vanish — and every downstream consumer quietly gets it wrong. A line chart connects across the hole and hides the outage. A 7-day moving average computed with `ROWS BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING` averages the last 7 *rows*, not the last 7 *days*, so a week with 3 zero-order days becomes an average over 10 calendar days. A "days since last purchase" calc undercounts. The absence of a row is data, and SQL's default is to throw it away.

The fix is a **date spine**: generate every date in the range, then `LEFT JOIN` your facts onto it.

```sql
WITH spine AS (
  SELECT generate_series(
    DATE '2026-01-01', DATE '2026-08-25', INTERVAL '1 day'
  )::date AS d          -- Postgres/DuckDB. BigQuery: GENERATE_DATE_ARRAY + UNNEST
),                       -- Snowflake: ROW_NUMBER over TABLE(GENERATOR(...))
daily AS (
  SELECT s.d,
         COALESCE(SUM(o.amount), 0) AS revenue,
         COUNT(o.order_id)          AS orders
  FROM spine s
  LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.order_date = s.d
  GROUP BY s.d
)
SELECT d, revenue,
       AVG(revenue) OVER (
         ORDER BY d ROWS BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW
       ) AS ma7
FROM daily ORDER BY d;
```

Two details that bite:

**`COALESCE` inside the aggregate, not around it.** `SUM(o.amount)` over zero matched rows returns `NULL`, not `0`. Wrap it or your moving average skips nulls and silently shortens its own window.

**Cross join for per-entity spines.** Retention and cohort curves need every (entity, date) pair, not just every date:

```sql
FROM spine s
CROSS JOIN (SELECT DISTINCT customer_id FROM orders) c
LEFT JOIN orders o
  ON o.order_date = s.d AND o.customer_id = c.customer_id
```

Watch the cardinality: 5,000 customers × 237 days = 1.2M rows before aggregation. Bound the spine to each entity's own active window (join on `s.d BETWEEN c.first_seen AND c.last_seen`) when the full grid is too wide.

Materialize a `dim_date` table once — with `is_weekend`, `fiscal_quarter`, `is_holiday` — and every analyst stops reinventing this.

## 10:02 - [ML & AI] Quantization: The Arithmetic of Running LLMs Locally

Quantization stores weights in fewer bits than the fp16 they were trained in. The memory math is the whole reason it matters:

```
weights_bytes ≈ params × (bits / 8)
```

Llama-3-8B at fp16: `8e9 × 2 = 16 GB` — won't fit a 12 GB card. At 4-bit: ~4 GB. A 70B model drops from 140 GB to ~40 GB, i.e. from a multi-GPU node to a single 48 GB card.

**"4-bit" is never exactly 4 bits.** Weights are quantized in blocks (typically 32–128 values) that share a scale factor: `w ≈ scale × q`, with `q` an int4 and `scale` stored at higher precision. Those per-block scales add overhead, which is why GGUF's `Q4_K_M` lands around 4.8 effective bits — an 8B model ships at ~4.9 GB, not 4.0.

**Don't forget the KV cache**, which quantization of weights does nothing for:

```
kv_bytes_per_token = 2 × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × bytes_per_elem
# Llama-3-8B: 2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 131 072 B ≈ 128 KB/token
```

At 8k context that's ~1.1 GB on top of the weights; at 32k, ~4.3 GB — often the thing that actually OOMs you.

Loading 4-bit weights in Transformers:

```python
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, BitsAndBytesConfig

cfg = BitsAndBytesConfig(
    load_in_4bit=True,
    bnb_4bit_quant_type="nf4",          # normal-float4, tuned for weight distributions
    bnb_4bit_compute_dtype="bfloat16",  # matmuls still run in bf16
    bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=True,     # quantize the scales too, saves ~0.4 bits/param
)
model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct",
                                             quantization_config=cfg, device_map="auto")
```

Note `compute_dtype`: weights are *stored* at 4 bits and dequantized on the fly. You save memory, not necessarily FLOPs — though for single-stream local inference, which is memory-bandwidth-bound, less data moved usually does mean faster tokens/sec.

**Practical picks.** GGUF `Q4_K_M` (llama.cpp, CPU/Metal/mixed) or AWQ/GPTQ (GPU batch serving) cost little measurable quality. Below ~3 bits, degradation gets real and shows up first on long-context reasoning and code, not on short chat prompts — so benchmark on your actual task, not on vibes. Given a fixed memory budget, a larger model at 4-bit generally beats a smaller one at fp16.

## 15:04 - [Crypto & Business] Cash Flow vs Profit: How a Growing, Profitable Company Runs Out of Money

Profit is an accounting opinion about *when* revenue and cost belong to a period. Cash is a fact about what's in the bank. Growth widens the gap between them, and the gap is what kills otherwise healthy businesses.

**The mechanism: the cash conversion cycle (CCC).**

```
CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO
```

- **DIO** — days inventory outstanding: how long cash sits as stock
- **DSO** — days sales outstanding: how long customers take to pay
- **DPO** — days payable outstanding: how long you take to pay suppliers

**Worked example.** A hardware reseller does $200k/month revenue at 25% gross margin, so $50k gross profit and $20k/month of fixed costs — $30k/month of accounting profit. Healthy on paper.

Now the timing. It buys inventory and holds it 45 days (DIO), invoices enterprise customers on net-60 who actually pay in 68 days (DSO), and its supplier demands payment in 30 (DPO).

```
CCC = 45 + 68 - 30 = 83 days
```

Every dollar of COGS is out the door 83 days before the matching cash comes back. At $150k/month COGS, that's roughly $150k × (83/30) ≈ **$415k of cash permanently tied up in working capital** just to run at current size.

Then sales double. COGS goes to $300k/month, working capital needed jumps to ~$830k — the business must fund an extra **$415k** out of a P&L generating $30k/month. It is more profitable and closer to insolvent at the same time. This is the classic *"growing broke."*

**Levers, in order of leverage:** cut DSO (deposits, milestone billing, 2/10 net-30 discounts, autopay), then DIO (drop-ship, consignment, tighter SKUs), then DPO (negotiate net-60 — but don't fund yourself by quietly stiffing suppliers).

**Practical habit:** forecast the cash balance weekly for 13 weeks, not monthly P&L. And know your own cash-out date — the single most useful number an operator carries in their head.
