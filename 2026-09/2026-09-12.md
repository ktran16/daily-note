## 00:03 - [Data Engineering] Airflow Concurrency: Why Your Task Sits in `queued` Forever

A task stuck in `queued` is not a broken worker — it means the scheduler already decided the task is runnable and something is throttling it. Airflow enforces **four independent limits**, and the tightest one wins. Debugging means finding which.

**1. Deployment-wide: `parallelism`** (default 32) caps running task instances across the entire scheduler.

```ini
# airflow.cfg
[core]
parallelism = 128
max_active_tasks_per_dag = 16
max_active_runs_per_dag = 16
```

**2. Per-DAG task limit: `max_active_tasks`** — even with 500 idle worker slots, one DAG runs at most this many tasks.

**3. Per-DAG run limit: `max_active_runs`** — the silent backfill killer. At `max_active_runs=1`, a 90-day backfill runs strictly serially no matter how wide your cluster is.

```python
with DAG(
    "orders_etl",
    schedule="@daily",
    max_active_tasks=32,
    max_active_runs=4,          # allow 4 logical dates in flight
    catchup=True,
) as dag:
    ...
```

**4. Pools** — the one people forget. Every task lands in `default_pool` (128 slots) unless assigned. Pools are how you protect a fragile upstream:

```python
extract = PythonOperator(
    task_id="extract_from_oracle",
    python_callable=extract,
    pool="oracle_legacy",   # pool created with 3 slots
    pool_slots=1,           # a heavy Spark task might claim 4
)
```

Now at most three tasks hit that database concurrently, cluster-wide, across every DAG.

**Two task-level limits worth knowing:** `max_active_tis_per_dag` caps copies of *one task* across all runs — essential when backfilling a task that writes to a shared partition. `max_active_tis_per_dagrun` does the same within a single run's mapped tasks.

**Diagnosing:** `scheduled` means Airflow hasn't picked it; `queued` means it's gated or waiting on an executor slot. Check Admin → Pools for used vs. queued slots, then work outward:

```sql
SELECT pool, state, count(*)
FROM task_instance
WHERE state IN ('queued','running')
GROUP BY 1,2;
```

If a pool shows 0 free slots, that's your answer. If pools look fine, you're hitting `parallelism` or Celery's `worker_concurrency`. Raising `parallelism` without raising worker capacity just moves the queue — size limits to what your *warehouse* tolerates, not what your scheduler can emit.

## 05:03 - [Data Analytics] Simpson's Paradox: Every Segment Improved and the Total Still Fell

You present Q2 conversion. Desktop is up. Mobile is up. Overall conversion dropped 1.4 points. Someone asks which number is wrong. Neither is.

| Quarter | Segment | Visits | Conv. | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | desktop | 900 | 90 | 10.0% |
| Q1 | mobile | 100 | 4 | 4.0% |
| **Q1** | **all** | **1000** | **94** | **9.4%** |
| Q2 | desktop | 400 | 44 | 11.0% |
| Q2 | mobile | 600 | 36 | 6.0% |
| **Q2** | **all** | **1000** | **80** | **8.0%** |

The overall rate is a *weighted average* of segment rates, and the weights moved: mobile went from 10% of traffic to 60%, and mobile converts worse. A marketing campaign shifted the traffic mix, and the mix swamped the genuine improvement inside each segment.

### Decompose instead of arguing

The change splits exactly into three terms (`w` = share of visits, `r` = segment rate):

`ΔR = Σ Δw·r₀ (mix) + Σ w₀·Δr (rate) + Σ Δw·Δr (interaction)`

```sql
WITH quarterly(quarter, device, visits, conversions) AS (
    VALUES ('Q1','desktop',900,90), ('Q1','mobile',100, 4),
           ('Q2','desktop',400,44), ('Q2','mobile',600,36)
),
seg AS (
    SELECT quarter, device,
           conversions * 1.0 / visits                            AS rate,
           visits * 1.0 / SUM(visits) OVER (PARTITION BY quarter) AS weight
    FROM quarterly
),
paired AS (
    SELECT a.weight AS w0, b.weight AS w1, a.rate AS r0, b.rate AS r1
    FROM seg a JOIN seg b ON a.device = b.device
    WHERE a.quarter = 'Q1' AND b.quarter = 'Q2'
)
SELECT ROUND(SUM((w1-w0) *  r0     ) * 100, 2) AS mix_pp,
       ROUND(SUM( w0     * (r1-r0) ) * 100, 2) AS rate_pp,
       ROUND(SUM((w1-w0) * (r1-r0) ) * 100, 2) AS interaction_pp
FROM paired;
-- mix_pp = -3.00 | rate_pp = +1.10 | interaction_pp = +0.50  →  sums to -1.40
```

The three terms sum to the actual change with no residual — it's an algebraic identity, not an approximation. Because segment weights always sum to 1, `Σ Δw = 0`, which is what makes the mix term interpretable on its own.

**In practice:** when the total and every segment disagree, mix is doing the work — lead with that, don't bury it. Report the decomposition rather than silently "controlling for" device, because the mix shift is usually the real business event. Two caveats: the paradox can only be detected on dimensions you actually collected, and a different split (channel, geo, new vs. returning) can flip the story again, so decompose on the dimension you believe drives acquisition — not the one that makes the number look best.
