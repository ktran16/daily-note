## 05:02 - [Data Analytics] Log-Scale Axes: Right for Growth, Wrong for Bars

On a linear axis a series going 10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 looks like nothing happened for three periods and then exploded. All four steps are the same event: 10x growth. A log y-axis makes equal *ratios* equal *distances*, so constant growth is a straight line and the slope *is* the growth rate.

Two products, 36 weeks — a small fast grower and a large slow one:

```python
import numpy as np, matplotlib.pyplot as plt

w = np.arange(36)
a = 1_000   * 1.25**w   # 25%/wk, starts at 1k
b = 400_000 * 1.03**w   #  3%/wk, starts at 400k

fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4))
for p in ax:
    p.plot(w, a, label="Product A"); p.plot(w, b, label="Product B")
    p.set_xlabel("week")
ax[0].set_title("linear")
ax[1].set_yscale("log"); ax[1].set_title("log")
ax[1].set_ylabel("users (log scale)"); ax[1].legend()
```

Linear panel: at week 18 A is at 55,511 against B's 680,973 — 2.25% of the axis height, a line pinned to the floor. The crossover at week 31 (1.01M vs 1.00M) is invisible until it happens.

Log panel: both are straight lines from week 0, A's visibly steeper. You can lay a ruler on them and read the crossover **before** you reach it. That extrapolation is the real payoff — and it only works because exponential growth is linear in log space.

**Four rules.**

1. **Never log a bar or area chart.** Bars encode value as length from zero; log breaks that, so a bar twice as long is not twice the value. Log belongs to position encodings only — lines and points.
2. **Label ticks in data units**: `1k, 10k, 100k, 1M`, not `3, 4, 5, 6`. Use `ax.yaxis.set_major_formatter(mpl.ticker.EngFormatter())`.
3. **Zeros and negatives vanish silently.** `log(0)` is undefined and matplotlib just drops those points — no warning, no gap you'd notice. Check `(s <= 0).sum()` first; for data that genuinely crosses zero use `symlog` and say so.
4. **Write "log scale" on the axis.** Readers default to linear, and a 2x jump that renders as a small bump is a real misread, not a subtle one.

The test: if your takeaway sentence says "**%**" or "**times**", log is probably right. If it says "**how much more**", stay linear.
